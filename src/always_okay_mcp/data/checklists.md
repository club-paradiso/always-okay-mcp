# Checklists

Working checklists derived from the principles (IDs in brackets). Use the one that matches the task.

## Contents

- Concept test
- Signature device test

- Launch plan check

## Brief interrogation [P01–P05]
- What is the reason this must exist, in one sentence? If the brief does not say, ask once or state an assumption.
- Which words in the brief are being obeyed unexamined ("trendy", "Gen Z", "premium", "MZ", "감성", "global")? Rewrite each as a question with a specific answer.
- What does the project already own: name, core activity, people's real traits, the archetype of its main object?
- Who does not care about this category yet? What true, specific emotion could reach them?
- What does the surrounding environment look and sound like? Where is the felt-but-unnamed gap?

## Concept test [P06–P11]
- Can the concept be said in one sentence that only this project could say?
- Does it come from the material's own first impression, not from a reference board?
- Does it cover the whole experience (material, image, product, launch, behaviour), not only visuals?
- Is there one deliberate mismatch, and an emotional balance of opposites?
- Is coherence carried by a reason that can be expressed differently each time — not by lore or a fixed look?
- Would it still feel good in ten years? Is any period look (retro, Y2K, film grain, uniforms) doing the idea's job? If yes, remove it or justify it as an ingredient.

## Signature device test [P06, P19, P21, P23]
- Is there one concrete device (object, mechanism, ritual, name, rule, moment) that carries the idea?
- Does it come from the user's material, not from a style or a past case?
- Would someone retell it tomorrow in one sentence?
- Does it appear in the product/service itself, in the launch order, in the copy and in the visuals?
- Is it specified enough to build: who, what, when, how many, what it says?

## Edit pass (critique) [P12–P14, P16, P18, P28]
1. Verdict first, in one line.
2. What works, briefly and specifically (no praise words).
3. What is formula or inertia: conventions kept without a reason; cosmetic newness.
4. What to subtract (list). Where is the gap the audience can enter?
5. Which visible elements carry no meaning?
6. Does anything declare its own quality or benefit? Rewrite to show it.
7. The single highest-leverage fix.

## Launch plan check [P21–P23, P20, P04]
- First encounter: what is met first, through which sense, at what hour, in what setting? Why?
- Reveal order: each step has a reason for its position.
- Withholding: what is held back, why it rewards rather than teases, and what must never be withheld (safety, price, terms, access).
- Advance description is capped so the work can exceed expectation.
- Participation: what can people do (decode, customise, copy, vary)? Optional and low-friction?
- Attention: is there existing attention or trust? If not, how is the first audience reached?
- Metrics: no inflated signals (random gacha, push-out sales, paid view farming).

## Briefing a specialist [P24–P27]
- Purpose and context; the reason behind the intended impression.
- At most one optional image; a few anchor items; constraints to drop.
- Withhold reference images where copying is the risk.
- What is theirs to decide (method, scenario, form).
- What conditions will produce the quality you want (time, trust, room to improvise)?
- How feedback will come: clear, sparing, direction-first.

## Convention audit [P16]
List the category's reflex moves. For each: keep (reason) · drop (reason) · change (how).
Never drop conventions that exist for accessibility, safety, law or trust.

## Taste tests (not looks)
Origin · mismatch · balance · subtraction · gap · density · meaning · newness · time · declaration.
See the questions in each section above. Taste is applied as tests to the user's material; no look is a default.
